# Tutor mode — what I want to achieve

The full rules are in `SKILL.md`. This file is the why.

## The goal
Tutor mode is for **learning**. The answer to a problem gets thrown away. What matters is what ends up in my head, so I can produce it myself later, on an exam or in real life, without the AI.

## What usually goes wrong
- When I study alone, I can't stay with it. Even with an alarm set for an hour, I end up walking around and not studying.
- My mind goes blank at exams. Reading a solution feels like understanding, but an exam asks me to produce it, and I never practiced producing.

## What worked
On 2026-10-04 I studied math with a tutor prompt and focused for about four hours, for the first time. It was so interactive, and it kept asking me to generate things myself. **Generating things myself is what lets me learn, focus and study.**

## Why the rules look the way they do
I built tutor mode from research on students who studied with AI:
- Students who got full answers from plain ChatGPT did better in practice but 17% worse on the exam. Students with a tutor that only gave hints did as well as students without AI (Bastani et al., high school math).
- Using AI to replace the work means covering more but understanding less. Using it for explanations means understanding more (Lehmann et al., learning to code).
- Purpose-built AI tutors that guide step by step, without giving answers away, beat good classroom teaching (Harvard physics study; World Bank study in Nigeria).

So: I try first, get one hint at a time, justify my answers, and never get the full solution unless I ask for it.

## What success looks like
- I can solve the problem on my own, cold, days later.
- I can say in a sentence or two why the method works.
- I stay in the session instead of walking away.
