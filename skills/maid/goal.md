# Maid mode — what I want to achieve

The full rules are in `SKILL.md`. This file is the why.

## The goal
Maid mode is for **making things**: stories, chatbots, video scripts, or any other project. It's not for learning. It's for doing and achieving what I want. The thing that matters is the result, and how well I do is measured by how much I produced, not by the quality.

I want the same experience I get from tutor mode (interactive, focused, small steps, quick feedback), but for making things instead of learning.

## What usually goes wrong
- The project is overwhelming at the start. I have too many things I want to do, I'm too ambitious, and I don't know where to start.
- I keep thinking about how to get the best result instead of just making something.
- Even telling the AI what to do is hard, because I want to do it well.

## What I want from it
- Get me started with the smallest step, the way the tutor asks small questions instead of one big one. Then keep me going.
- Ask me what I want to do. A general description from me is enough, then ask specifically until I've actually shared what I want. **My own ideas come first.**
- After that, the AI can write for me and do the work. I mainly want the guiding process.
- Make what I enjoy, by my own taste, not what's popular or what other people make.

## Why the rules look the way they do
- When the AI writes first, people stop feeling the work is theirs. When they put in their own ideas first, the result is better and stays theirs (MIT, "Your Brain on ChatGPT").
- AI goal coaches help people reach their goals, about as well as human coaches (several randomized trials).
- When someone stalls, showing them a draft of the next piece gets them back to work faster than encouragement does (CatAlyst).
- If-then plans ("when X happens, I'll do Y") roughly double follow-through (Gollwitzer & Sheeran).
- Procrastination is about escaping a bad feeling, not laziness, and guilt makes it worse (Sirois & Pychyl; Wohl et al.).
- Too many questions can become a new way to stall (Bhattacharjee et al.).

## What success looks like
- Things get made and the pile grows.
- I start without being stuck at the beginning.
- What I make feels like mine.
